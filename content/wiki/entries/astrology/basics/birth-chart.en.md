# How to Read a Birth Chart

## What an Astrology Chart Is

An astrology chart, also called a natal chart or birth chart, is a snapshot of the sky for a birth date, time, and place. It maps the zodiac, planets, horizon, meridian, houses, and aspects onto one wheel. Western astrology commonly uses the tropical zodiac, anchored to seasonal points rather than the current visible constellation background.

A chart does not sort people into twelve personalities. It is a structured set of clues: a planet’s energy takes a sign’s style, appears in a house’s life area, and makes cooperative, tense, or adjusting relationships with other factors.

## Start With the Big Three

- **Sun**: core identity, vitality, and long-term growth direction. This is the “sign” people usually ask about.
- **Moon**: emotional needs, security, care, instincts, and private reactions.
- **Rising sign**: the zodiac degree rising on the eastern horizon at birth. It shapes embodiment, first impressions, and the 1st-house cusp.

The elements and modes of these three points often reveal more than a Sun sign alone.

## Planets and Topics

Mercury relates to thinking and language; Venus to value, attraction, and relationships; Mars to action and desire. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto describe growth, structure, disruption, dissolution, and deep transformation. Personal planets often show immediate behavior; outer planets work across longer cycles.

## Houses and Aspects

The house answers where a planet acts. The sign answers how it acts. Aspects answer how chart factors interact. Conjunctions concentrate energy; sextiles and trines ease coordination; squares create pressure that can motivate action; oppositions ask for balance between two poles.

House rulers add detail. If Capricorn is on the 10th-house cusp, Saturn rules the career house. Saturn’s sign, house, and aspects then describe more of that vocational story.

## A Four-Step Reading

1. Read the Sun, Moon, and rising sign for core needs and outward pacing.
2. Find the strongest planets and any clustered signs or houses.
3. Read placements by sign and house, then add major aspects and rulers.
4. Return to the whole chart: repeated themes, resources, pressures, and growth points.
